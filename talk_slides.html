The gap

A finding without a reason

Haberman & Kelsay (2021) found ~4.5% fewer robberies per percentage point of street grade in Cincinnati. They closed by naming three possible reasons:

  • Physical cost — climbing and hauling take energy
  • Escape difficulty — steep blocks are hard to leave
  • Lower usage — fewer people pass through

Three mechanisms, one observed sign, no test between them. Nobody has run that test since.

Prior work disagrees on which terrain measure even matters. Breetzke: altitude yes, slope no. Kim & Wo: all measures, hilliness strongest. Two 2026 bicycle-theft papers split the same way.

Poisson pseudo-ML — multiplicative mean, many zeros, consistent under any variance (Santos Silva & Tenreyro). Exposure E enters as an offset: housing units + population, apportioned within block group by residential building footprint area, not land area. αc absorbed ppmlhdfe-style; SEs clustered on block group.

Identification is within block group. Everything constant across a neighbourhood — income, zoning, beat, reporting regime — is differenced out. What is not removed: anything varying inside one that tracks slope. That boundary governs every claim that follows.

Result 1

It generalizes — and the magnitude does not travel

All nine negative and significant. Random-effects pool over the four higher-gradient cities, REML τ² with Hartung–Knapp:

−6.89% [−9.51, −4.21] per degree
  • I² = 0.75, but its Q-profile interval is [0.19, 0.98] — at k=4 you learn heterogeneity exists and almost nothing about its size
  • Prediction interval [−13.7%, +0.5%]. A tenth city could plausibly show nothing
  • Fixed-effect pooling gave [−7.39, −5.82]. Same point estimate, half the honest width
Haberman & Kelsay convert to −7.72%/degree — inside our range, from a different crime type, city, decade and estimator.

Result 2 · measurement

Terrain measurability, not a threshold

Low-relief cities returned implausibly large effects — Chicago, at 0.93° of slope variation, gave −19.8%/degree.

Why: below ~3°, a bare-earth 10 m DEM is measuring crowned roadbeds, embankments and railway cuts. None of that is terrain; all of it tracks land use.

The first draft cut the sample at 3°. That number came from looking at the data. Replaced by a random-effects meta-regression on all nine:

+4.06% per degree of slope SD
[+0.84, +7.38] p = 0.020 R² = 0.59

Same claim, no sample cut, and now a continuous prediction that can be checked.

Mechanism 1 · the core test

Crimes that carry nothing away

Vandalism and arson are property crimes where nothing is removed. Approach costs what it costs a burglar; departure costs nothing extra.

If the association is the metabolic cost of hauling loot along a gradient, theft must be markedly more slope-sensitive. Estimated on the same cells, same block groups, same covariates, with the difference bootstrapped over block groups so the correlation between the two coefficients is carried through.

A null is not equivalence. TOST against a pre-set margin of half the headline effect (3.48 pp/degree): p < 10⁻⁵.

What that test does and does not kill

ruled out The association is driven mainly by the metabolic cost of moving stolen property along a gradient.

still live Cost of approaching a target. Perceived effort. Search costs. Escape risk. A visual heuristic — hills simply look remote or exposed at the moment of target selection.

Earlier drafts called this a placebo. It isn't one. Vandalism, arson and theft differ in offender age, motive, planning, co-offending, tooling and reporting reliability. It is a negative control for one channel, and the paper now says only that.

Mechanism 2

Target scarcity: the objection that should have worked

The strongest alternative is definitional. If steep cells simply hold fewer parked cars and front doors per unit of measured housing, the whole result is a denominator artifact.

San Francisco can test this directly: the SFMTA on-street parking census (275,339 surveyed spaces, 14,346 blocks, no fallback) plus the city address-point file.

The objection fails before a crime model is fitted. Regressing target counts on slope, steep streets carry more targets per housing unit:

Target%/degree95% CI
Parking spaces+2.16[+1.55, +2.77]
Front doors+1.80[+0.96, +2.64]
Buildings ≤15 m+3.76[+2.78, +4.74]

Raw parking density barely moves with gradient — 16.3 spaces per 100 m below 1°, 17.6 at 6–12°. What changes is housing density.

San Francisco's hills do not lose parked cars.
They lose apartments.

Swap the denominator and the association roughly doubles — negative shrinkage in all forty comparisons. Residential burglary, null under housing exposure, becomes significant under front doors.

Consequence: the housing-denominator estimates are conservative.

One city. The national extension on building footprints fails to converge in five of nine and is uninformative in the rest — reported as such.

Mechanisms 3 & 4

Network structure and affluence

Street network — the best survivor

Betweenness, intersection density, permeability, egress count, walk/drive ratio, on street segments in four cities. Pooled attenuation 24.4%.

Pre-specified 40% threshold. We no longer treat that as a verdict — the number was arbitrary, and coefficient attenuation is not a causal indirect effect: no defined estimand, no assumption of unconfounded mediator, and in a nonlinear model the change isn't a clean proportion.

Betweenness is topological potential, not measured usage. Distance-weighted, its top San Francisco streets are ridge lanes — terrain forcing paths through them. Travel-time weighting gives freeways instead; ρ = 0.68. A viewshed proxy is collinear with terrain (r = 0.80), so visibility is untested, not rejected.

Affluence — a claim we retracted

An earlier draft said affluence "fails by construction." It doesn't.

  • Income, value, tenure, vacancy are measured at block-group level — the fixed effects absorb them; the covariates do almost no work
  • Within one block group, household wealth, security hardware, housing type and propensity to report can all still track slope
  • Ye & Becker (2018) document exactly this vertical sorting across 17 US cities — a documented regularity, not a hypothetical

What survives: the association is not driven by between-neighbourhood sorting. That is a real result, and less than ruling affluence out.

Limits

What would actually settle this

The binding constraint is data, not method. Municipal portals publish incidents, not offenders. We have no residences, no cleared-offence linkage, and no observed movement.

  • Observed foot and vehicle counts — the usage mechanism is the most plausible survivor and the least evidenced
  • Discrete choice over places not chosen, with offender origins (Frith, Johnson & Fry; Johnson & Summers; Clare et al.) — the design that identifies the mechanism
  • Quasi-experiments on access — Pittsburgh's public staircases; Medellín's gondolas and escalators
  • Perception experiments — separating actual metabolic cost from a visual hill heuristic

Standing limitations we don't argue with:

  • Observational. Fixed effects remove between-neighbourhood variation, not within
  • Slope replaced relative height after seeing data — the headline is a discovery, not a confirmatory test
  • Reported crime, not victimisation. Reporting rises with income; income rises with elevation
  • Target-count denominators rest on one city
  • I² interval [0.19, 0.98]; 41% of between-city variance unexplained

The claim

A robust association that outlives its explanations

Across nine American cities, steeper streets carry measurably less recorded property crime within the same neighbourhood — about 7% per degree where terrain is well measured.

It is not the cost of carrying loot. It is not between-neighbourhood wealth. It is not target scarcity. Street layout accounts for a quarter.

The remainder is unexplained, and the most likely candidate is the one thing this design cannot measure.

Ending with an unidentified mechanism is scientifically acceptable. Claiming to have rejected mechanisms more decisively than the design permits is not — which is the difference between this version and the last one.
